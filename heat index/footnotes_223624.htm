Why was the data set created?

Purpose

  • Identify and prioritize areas that may require special attention during the heat season or additional action to reduce heat-related illness over time
  • Educate and inform the public about heat risk in their community
  • Analyze the unique, local factors driving heat-related illness to inform policy and decision-making

How was the data set created?

Derivation of measure

The Heat and Health Index (HHI) delivers a single (percentile) rank for each ZIP code so that public health officials, city planners, policymakers, and community members can identify and map areas that may experience negative health outcomes from heat events across the country. In addition to including historical heat-related illness data, the HHI incorporates data on pre-existing health conditions, sociodemographic factors, and natural and built environment factors, to assess vulnerability to heat events. 

The HHI comprises four modules:

  1. A Historical Heat and Health Burden module which focuses on localized measures of previous experience with heat events.
  2. A Sensitivity module comprised of pre-existing health conditions that may increase negative health outcomes when the individual with the condition is exposed to heat events.
  3. A Sociodemographic module encompassing social and demographic characteristics that increase exposure or sensitivity to heat or lessen one’s ability to cope with heat events.
  4. A Natural and Built Environment module which focuses on characteristics of the natural and built environment that increase exposure or sensitivity to heat or lessen one’s ability to cope with heat events.

Review the HHI Technical Documentation for more details on the derivation of the HHI modules and percentile ranking calculations.

What are the limitations of the data set?

  • The HHI relies on historical data generated by various institutions on varying time scales, meaning that the HHI is not entirely reflective of current or future conditions.
  • Many indicators used to construct the HHI rely on estimates that involve some level of uncertainty. When possible, the HHI should be supplemented with local data.
  • ZCTAs with an estimated population of less than 50 residents were excluded from the index. Population size exclusion criteria was used due to the likelihood of low population areas having higher margins of error for variable estimations and as a measure of suppression to prevent attempts at identifying individual residents. The cut-off of excluding ZCTAs with populations of less than 50 residents was selected to align with the cut-off that CDC PLACES uses for their ZCTA estimates. Visit https://www.cdc.gov/places/index.html to learn more about CDC PLACES methods.
  • The heat-related illness data are based on emergency medical services (EMS) activations and are subject to several limitations, including restrictions on accessing raw counts or rates due to data use agreements. Additionally, EMS activations are not a comprehensive representation of all heat-related emergency health events or all heat-related non-emergent health events. For example, this data does not include emergency department visits or urgent care visits for heat-related illness, nor does it include heat-related illnesses where treatment was not sought.
  • Health indicators represented within the Sensitivity Module of the HHI are derived from CDC PLACES estimates produced by the Division of Population Health within the CDC’s National Center for Chronic Disease Prevention and Health Promotion (NCCDPHP). Users who wish to view more detailed estimates of chronic disease prevalence or learn more about the small area estimation techniques used to produce the PLACES estimates should visit https://www.cdc.gov/places/index.html.
  • A lack of data for some indicators led to the exclusion of Alaska, Hawaii, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and all other Island Territories (the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands, Guam) from the current HHI calculations. The HHI currently includes only the Continental U.S. (48 states and the District of Columbia).

Refer to the 2024 HHI Technical Documentation for more details on data limitations.

How should these data be used?

Use Constraints

The HHI is intended as a high-level mapping tool to help identify and prioritize areas across the country where people are most likely to experience harmful health effects from heat events. However, like any tool, the HHI is subject to several limitations that should govern proper use of the tool.

 

The HHI is not intended as the following

  • A definitive tool for determining which communities will and won’t be affected by heat events
  • A tool for characterizing how likely an individual is to experience heat-related illness
  • A comprehensive representation of current and/or future heat, heat-related illness, pre-existing health conditions, sociodemographic, or natural and built environmental characteristics

Refer to the 2024 HHI Technical Documentation for more details on measure limitations.
