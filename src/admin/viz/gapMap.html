Resilience Gap Map

Question: is resilience investment reaching the Miami-Dade neighborhoods that are most exposed or most vulnerable? Each census tract is scored on need (the measure you pick below) and on investment per resident, then colored by the combination.

What one tract shows

A census tract is a Census Bureau neighborhood of roughly 2,000–8,000 people. Miami-Dade has 706 with land, of which 698 have residents. Each one gets two scores:

  1. Need: the measure picked in the left panel (sea level rise, storm surge, FEMA loss or risk, social vulnerability, or poverty).
  2. Investment per person: resilience project dollars assigned to the tract, divided by its residents.

Each score is split into three equal tiers within Miami-Dade (low, medium, high). The tract color is the combination of the two tiers. "High" therefore means the top third of this county, not high on any national scale.

The colors

  • Red, the gap. Top third for need, bottom third for investment per person.
  • Dark plum, matched. High need and high investment.
  • Teal. Low need, high investment.
  • Near-white. Low need, low investment.
  • Dark gray. No residents (airport, port, parkland), so no per-person value.

The 3D columns

  • One column per populated tract, standing inside the tract.
  • Color is the same 9-color key.
  • Height is the tract's rank within the county on the measure chosen in "Column height shows". A tract at the 90th percentile is about 90% as tall as the tallest.
  • Heights are exaggerated for visibility and carry no physical units. Twice as tall means a higher rank, not twice the value.
  • Tracts scoring zero (e.g. no 3 ft sea level rise exposure) have no column, only ground color.

With the default settings, look for tall red columns: highly exposed tracts with little investment per resident.

The white dots (2D)

Individual projects that are completed, ongoing or funded. Click one for its type, status, scale and cost. Planned projects are not shown.

How the map is built

  1. Project dollars are assigned to tracts by the SCALE-R tract analysis (12 Sep 2026) using each project's spatial scale: parcel, neighborhood and corridor projects go entirely to the tract containing the project point. Municipality projects are shared across that municipality's tracts and county projects across all tracts, both weighted by population.
  2. Investment per person = assigned dollars ÷ ACS 2020–2024 population.
  3. Need measures are joined by 11-digit tract GEOID. All 706 tracts match.
  4. Both scores are cut into county terciles among populated tracts. Where a third or more of tracts are exactly zero (sea level rise, storm surge), "low" means zero and the remaining tracts are split at their median.

Questions for review

1. Is the FEMA composite risk score appropriate here? The team's NRI audit recommends against it for analysis: FEMA builds it from expected annual loss, CDC social vulnerability and Census community resilience, so it mixes hazard with vulnerability. Expected annual loss is offered as the hazard-only alternative.
2. Are terciles the right cut-offs? The "gap" count depends on them. Alternatives: quartiles, fixed thresholds, or national percentiles.
3. County-wide projects. Spreading county projects by population raises every tract slightly and may understate local benefit near where the work is done.
4. Projects without a cost contribute $0, and regional systems (canals, pumps) protect tracts beyond the one they sit in.
5. Mixed data years. Population is ACS 2020–2024, poverty is SVI 2022 (ACS 2018–2022), FEMA NRI is the December 2025 file. Acceptable for a draft but should be stated in any write-up.
6. Tracts with no residents (e.g. Virginia Key). Nine Census special land-use tracts (98xx) have zero population: PortMiami, Virginia Key + Crandon Park, the airports, Everglades and Biscayne national parks, Turkey Point. FEMA and NOAA data exist for them, but investment per person is undefined, so they are shown dark gray. Virginia Key alone holds 10 projects / $62.5M (Marine Stadium restoration). Show them by total or per-acre investment instead?
7. Snapshot. Tract investment is a 12 Sep 2026 snapshot. Project dots load live, so edits made after that date appear as dots but not in the colors until the analysis is re-run.
